# ScreenClip 1.2.0

You can report a screenshot you took earlier on your iPhone or iPad. Your app can supply its own diagnostic files, and a coding agent can suggest project settings for you to review on the Mac.

## New

- In the iOS report sheet, tap the photo button to choose an existing screenshot. The Mac shows that it came from Photos and includes its capture date when available. The live screen’s view tree is omitted for these reports.
- Apps can use `ScreenClipKit.setAttachmentProvider` to attach text or JSON files. **Attach logs and diagnostics** controls whether providers run and whether their files are sent. A report takes up to 16 attachments of 1 MB each. [SDK guide](https://screenclipai.com/ios)
- Claude Code and Codex can propose a validation command or base branch. Review the suggestion in the menu bar popover or the project’s settings, then **Accept** or **Reject** it. Accepting a validation command continues reports waiting for one.

## Fixes

- Pull request descriptions keep their Before and After screenshots. ScreenClip removes its evidence folder from the pull request head before merging. When you merge by hand, you confirm again for that commit.
- Captures preserve the app’s contents when a transparent system window covers the scene, including on iPad.
- Local fixes now incorporate the latest base branch before project validation. A conflict goes back to the agent, and a binary conflict stays open until it is resolved. Nothing is left in your repository’s stash list.
- The menu bar shows a red dot when a report or project setting needs attention. Project settings fit within the sheet, including longer translated labels.
- Reinstalling a Debug app on the same device preserves its pairing when the pairing keys remain in the Keychain.
- The iOS composer keeps the note above the keyboard and provides larger annotation controls. On iOS 26 and later, exported screenshots follow the device’s rounded corners. While a screenshot from Photos loads, you can cancel it.

## Install

If you have 1.0.3 or later, ScreenClip installs this update on its own: it shows **Update to 1.2.0** in the menu bar popover once it is downloaded, or installs it the next time ScreenClip quits. **Check for Updates…** in the popover's **⋯** menu checks right away.

From 1.0.2 or earlier, download **ScreenClip.dmg** from this release, open it, and drag **ScreenClip** into **Applications**, replacing the older version. Your queue, projects and settings are kept. Requirements are unchanged: Apple Silicon and macOS 14 or later for the app, and iOS 17 or later for ScreenClipKit.

For your iOS app, update the Swift package `https://github.com/lludo/ScreenClip`, product **ScreenClipKit**, to version 1.2.0, and use it with ScreenClip 1.2.0 on the Mac. Devices paired with 1.1.0 stay paired. The [iOS guide](https://github.com/lludo/ScreenClip/blob/main/docs/ios-sdk.md) and the [SDK documentation](https://screenclipai.com/sdk/documentation/screenclipkit) have the details.

The app and disk image are signed with Developer ID, notarized by Apple, and stapled. Updates are also signed with ScreenClip's update key, and the app refuses any that are not. To verify the download, place `ScreenClip.dmg` and `SHA256SUMS` in the same folder and run:

```sh
shasum -a 256 -c SHA256SUMS
```
